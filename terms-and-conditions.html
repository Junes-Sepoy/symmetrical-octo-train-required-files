Confidentiality

The user, upon accessing counselling services, is entitled to protection of confidential information shared with the therapist. Therapy is a safe space, and the right to confidentiality is an integral part of this safe space. The conversations between a therapist and client constitute privileged information, meaning that the contents of these interactions are not revealed to anybody.

  • All therapists at Kaha Mind are bound by rules of confidentiality.
  • All administrative staff handling client information have been trained to protect user information and are bound by rules of confidentiality.

Limitations to confidentiality include:

  • Imminent danger to self or others: when there is evidence of threat or harm to self or others, therapists are required to disclose such information to the emergency contact shared by the client and/or law enforcement agencies to help prevent loss of life.
  • Nominated representative: disclosure to a nominated representative, if applicable.
  • Statutory authorities: in the rare event that a statutory authority such as the High Court, Supreme Court, a Board or other authority warrants disclosure of privileged information in order to ascertain the client’s mental status.
  • Other healthcare providers: disclosure to providers such as psychiatrists and GPs to inform and facilitate treatment.
  • Public safety: disclosure in the interest of public safety and security.

Only such information as is relevant for safeguarding life will be shared with legal authorities, if the contingency arises. As far as possible, every attempt will be made to discuss the step of disclosure to the emergency contact with the client beforehand.

Our confidentiality policy is in accordance with the Mental Healthcare Act, 2017 and is subject to change to reflect changes in legislation and/or policy.
